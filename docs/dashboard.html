Running your screens

Using the dashboard

The dashboard is where you decide what the TVs show. Open it on any phone, tablet or laptop on the club’s network — at http://pitchtribe.local, or whatever name you gave the Pi — and sign in with the admin password.

It has five sections, along the top:

SectionWhat it’s for
ContentEverything that can appear on a screen: photos, videos, notices, menus, fixtures, widgets, live scores and web pages.
PlaylistsThe running order for a screen, how long each item shows, and when.
ScreensEvery TV connected to PitchTribe, and which playlist each one plays.
Wi-FiThe optional guest Wi-Fi portal.
SettingsClub name, screen language, sports feeds, weather location and the admin password.

Changes reach the screens within a second or two. You never need to touch the Pi.

Content

Use the buttons at the top of the Content page to add something new. Notices, menus, fixture lists, widgets, live scores and web pages have an Add to playlist box — leave it on your playlist and the item goes straight on the screens.

Photos and videos

Tap Upload (or drag files onto the page on a computer). JPG, PNG, GIF, WebP and SVG pictures work, and MP4 and WebM videos. Then add them to a playlist (below).

  • Make pictures landscape, 1920 × 1080 for a normal TV. Canva and PowerPoint both have a 16:9 size.
  • Each item has a Sizing choice: Fit whole picture (may leave bars at the sides) or Fill screen (may crop the edges).
  • For a video, set Show for to 0 to play the whole thing, and choose whether to mute it.

Notices

A headline and a message in big, clear type, in one of five colours. Good for opening times, events and reminders. Your club name appears on the slide.

Menu boards

Type or paste your menu, one item per line, as name | price | notes. Start each section with #, and add | sold out to grey an item out. You can paste straight from a spreadsheet.

# Draught
Lager (pint) | £4.50
Real ale (pint) | £4.80 | Ask for today’s guest ale

# Food
Beef burger & chips | £8.50 | Contains gluten
Chicken & mushroom pie | £7.00 | | sold out

Fixture lists

One match per line: date and time | match | competition | venue. Dates can be written 09/11/2026 14:00 or 2026-11-09 14:00. Matches disappear by themselves three hours after kick-off, and the next one gets a countdown.

09/11/2026 14:00 | Riverside FC v Rovers United | League | Home
12/11/2026 19:30 | Training · Astroturf Pitch A
16/11/2026 15:00 | City Athletic v Riverside FC | League | Away

Live scores

Fixtures, live scores and results from the Premier League, Championship, Scottish Premiership, Champions League, NFL and NBA. First choose the competitions in Settings → Sports feeds, then add a Live scores item. Scores update every minute while matches are on.

The scores come from a free public feed meant for personal and non-commercial use. See data sources and licences.

Widgets

Ready-made slides that fill themselves in. Tap Widgets to see them all, including:

  • Time and weather: clock or countdown to kick-off, world clocks, weather for your ground, sunrise and sunset.
  • Out and about: a transport hub (buses, trains, London tube and roads, planes overhead), flood warnings, bank holidays, how green the electricity is right now.
  • Quizzes and fun: multiple-choice quiz, emoji quiz, flags and capitals, jokes, club phrases, and spot the ball with your own photo.
  • Knowledge: did you know?, on this day, born on this day, country of the day, news headlines and calendar events.

Quizzes, jokes and trivia are built in and work without the internet; a fresh selection is picked each day. Weather needs a location, set in Settings → Widgets.

Web pages

Shows any web page full screen — your club website, a league table, a live stream page. Some websites refuse to be shown inside another page; if yours stays blank, that site doesn’t allow it.

Playlists

A playlist is the running order for a screen. Your dashboard starts with one called Main, and every new screen plays it. Open it from Playlists.

  • Add content adds items from your Content library.
  • Drag items to reorder them (on a phone, use the up and down arrows).
  • Change the seconds next to an item to show it for longer or shorter.
  • The on/off switch takes an item off the screens without deleting it.
  • On-screen extras adds a clock in the corner and a scrolling ticker along the bottom — one message per line.
  • Preview shows what the screen will look like.

When you’ve made changes, tap Save changes.

Showing things only at certain times

Open an item’s Timing to choose when it’s allowed to show:

  • Dates — from and until (for an event, or an offer that ends).
  • Days of the week — none selected means every day.
  • Times of day — these can run past midnight, like 18:00 to 01:00.

The shortcut buttons fill these in for you: Always, Weekdays, Weekends, Lunch 12–2, Evenings 5pm–close and Today only. Each item then shows whether it’s Live now, Scheduled, Off-hours or Expired. A typical use: the lunch menu shows 12–2, the evening menu from 5pm, and the match-day notice only on Saturdays.

Different screens, different content

Create another playlist with New playlist — say “Bar” and “Changing rooms” — and assign each screen the one it should play (below).

Screens

Every TV running PitchTribe appears here by itself the first time it starts, named after its Pi. For each screen you can:

  • see whether it’s Online, and its resolution and address;
  • choose its Playlist;
  • set its Rotation if the TV is mounted on its side (portrait);
  • set its Language — dates, times and built-in labels in English, French, Spanish, German, Italian, Portuguese, Polish, Russian, Chinese or Japanese;
  • Identify it — flashes its name on the TV, handy when you have several;
  • Reload it remotely, or Remove one you no longer use.

The Add a screen box at the top explains how to add a TV that isn’t a Pi — see Adding more screens.

Who can see the dashboard

PitchTribe is built for a club’s own network, and that assumption is worth stating plainly rather than leaving you to discover it.

  • Anyone on the club’s Wi-Fi can reach the dashboard address — they still need the admin password to change anything, and the screens themselves need no password at all, by design, so a TV can come back on its own after a power cut.
  • Guests on the guest Wi-Fi cannot. The guest network is kept separate from the club network and from the Pi’s dashboard. See Guest Wi-Fi.
  • Over plain http://, your password and your signed-in session travel across the Wi-Fi unencrypted. On a network where everyone shares one Wi-Fi password, another device that knows that password can read them. For most clubs that is an acceptable trade for a dashboard that needs no setup — but it is a trade, not an oversight.

Turning on HTTPS

From v0.4.2, the Pi also answers on https://pitchtribe.local, using a certificate it generates for itself on first start. That encrypts the password and the session, so neither can be read off the Wi-Fi. Nothing is forced: http:// keeps working unless you switch the redirect on in Settings → Secure connection.

Your browser will warn you once — “Not private”, with a tap to continue. That is unavoidable and not a fault: no certificate authority will vouch for a name like pitchtribe.local that means something different on every network. The warning is about the certificate being unvouched-for, not about the connection being unencrypted.

Make that warning mean something

Settings → Secure connection shows the certificate’s fingerprint. Before you tap “continue”, open the warning’s details in your browser and compare the first few characters. If they match, you are talking to your Pi. If they ever stop matching and nobody reinstalled the Pi, stop and ask. Tapping through without looking is the part worth avoiding.

The TVs stay on http:// deliberately. A screen is a browser with no keyboard, so it has no way to dismiss a certificate warning — redirecting the screens would blank every TV in the club at once. They carry nothing private. If a phone will not get past the warning, http://pitchtribe.local/admin/?nossl gets you back.

Tips from clubs

  • Less is more. 4–6 items of 10–15 seconds each keeps people watching. Nobody reads a 40-second notice.
  • Big text. If you can’t read it from across the bar, it’s too small.
  • Use timings rather than deleting and re-adding things: the Christmas notice can sit there all year and only appear in December.
  • Several volunteers can use the dashboard at the same time from their own phones.
